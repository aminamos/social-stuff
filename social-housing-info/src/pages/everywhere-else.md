# Everywhere Else

New York gets the headlines, but some of the most innovative housing policy in the country is happening in smaller cities and states. Community land trusts, limited-equity cooperatives, and public development agencies are building permanently affordable housing outside the private market — and they're scaling.

## What is a community land trust?

A **community land trust (CLT)** is a nonprofit organization that acquires land and holds it in trust permanently. The CLT owns the land; residents own (or rent) the buildings on it. When a homeowner sells, the resale price is restricted by a formula — keeping the home affordable for the next buyer, forever. There are roughly **314 CLTs across 46 states**, holding about **44,000 permanently affordable units**.

## Hawaii

Hawaii has the nation's most acute affordability crisis — and one of the most active CLT movements.

- **PAL Hawai‘i (Ho‘omaluhia CLT)** and **Kohala CLT** are acquiring land and developing homes with perpetual affordability restrictions called *kama‘āina easements*.
- The **Lahaina CLT** (formed after the 2023 wildfires) uses a weighted lottery to prioritize long-time residents displaced by the fire — ensuring recovery doesn't become a land grab.
- **Nā Hale O Maui** broke ground on seven permanently affordable four-bedroom homes in Kahului (April 2026).
- **Governor Josh Green** signed Act 121 (June 2026), expanding the 99-year leasehold program to open more state and county land for affordable homeownership.
- The Hawaii legislature has introduced bills to give CLTs a line of credit (HB 833) and a right of first refusal on foreclosed homes (SB 332).

## Seattle & Washington State

Seattle is investing heavily in permanently affordable homeownership through CLTs and limited-equity cooperatives.

- **Homestead Community Land Trust** completed 30 condos in Phinney Ridge (June 2026) — 19 permanently affordable, with resale prices around $250,000–$335,000 (roughly half the area median).
- The **City of Seattle** awarded $7 million in 2025 for 51 permanently affordable homes in the Central District and South Park, plus $3.6 million for 24 homes in Rainier Valley.
- **Frolic Community** is developing Marra Commons, a 17-unit limited-equity cooperative in South Park.
- Mayor Harrell's proposed 2026 budget includes a record **$350 million** for affordable housing and a new **Anti-Displacement and Reparations Housing Fund**.
- State Senator Emily Alvarado and Governor Bob Ferguson have endorsed the CLT model as replicable statewide.

## California

- **Bay Area CLTs** (Oakland, San Francisco, East Palo Alto) have been operating for decades and are now expanding into new construction rather than just acquisition/rehab.
- The **Oakland Community Land Trust** has preserved over 30 properties since 2009, primarily through tenant-organized purchases of distressed buildings.
- California's **Community Land Trust Network** connects over 25 CLTs across the state, sharing legal templates, financing models, and policy advocacy.

## Other notable initiatives

- **Champlain Housing Trust (Vermont)**: The largest CLT in the country, with over 2,200 permanently affordable homes. Founded in 1984, it's the model most new CLTs look to.
- **Dudley Neighbors Inc. (Boston)**: A CLT that grew out of the Dudley Street Neighborhood Initiative — resident-led planning and community ownership of land in Roxbury.
- **Atlanta Land Trust**: Developing permanently affordable homes along the Atlanta BeltLine to prevent displacement as the corridor develops.
- **City of Lakes CLT (Minneapolis)**: Active in the same city that passed rent stabilization in 2021 — pairing tenant protections with community-owned land.

## The public developer model

Several cities are experimenting with **municipal development corporations** — publicly owned entities that build housing directly, bypassing private developers entirely:

- **Montgomery County, Maryland**: The Housing Opportunities Commission acts as a public developer, building mixed-income apartments where market-rate units cross-subsidize affordable ones.
- **Rhode Island**: A proposed statewide public developer would build mixed-income housing on state-owned land, with a mandate to prioritize permanently affordable units.
- **NYC Block by Block**: Mamdani's plan includes a municipal development corporation — see the <a href="/block-by-block">NYC Block by Block</a> page.

## Why CLTs matter for the rent control debate

The most common objection to rent stabilization is that it discourages private development. CLTs and public developers are the answer: if the private market won't build affordable housing, **public and community institutions can**. They decouple housing from speculation — homes stay affordable permanently because the land is never sold at market rate. And they've been doing it quietly, successfully, for decades.
