# NYC Block by Block

In May 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani released **"Block by Block: The Housing Plan for a New Era"** — a 112-page blueprint to dramatically accelerate affordable housing production across New York City while strengthening tenant protections and construction labor standards.

The plan is the **development counterpart** to the rent freeze: if the freeze protects existing tenants, Block by Block aims to build the housing stock the city desperately needs.

## The headline numbers

- **200,000 new affordable homes** built over the next decade
- **200,000 existing affordable homes** preserved or stabilized
- **$22 billion** in city capital investment over five years
- **$5.6 billion** to improve NYCHA (public housing) campuses
- **$28 billion** total package — the largest housing investment in NYC history

## Key components

### Municipal development corporation

The city will act as its **own developer**, bypassing private developers on city-owned land. This is the core idea behind "block by block": using vacant and underutilized city-owned lots across the five boroughs to build mixed-income, permanently affordable housing at the neighborhood scale.

### Construction Justice Act

A **$40/hour minimum wage** for all construction workers on city-financed projects, plus benefits and project labor agreements for targeted affordable housing developments. This addresses the frequent criticism that affordable housing means cheap labor — Mamdani's framework ties affordability for tenants to dignity for workers.

### Code enforcement crackdown

Aggressive enforcement against negligent landlords, including:
- Expanded inspection programs
- Higher fines for repeat offenders
- Accelerated timelines for court-ordered repairs
- A publicly accessible landlord violation database

### NYCHA renewal

The New York City Housing Authority — the nation's largest public housing agency, serving ~400,000 residents — gets $5.6 billion for campus improvements, new financing tools to unlock federal funds, and a mandate to act as a **public developer** building new mixed-income housing on NYCHA land.

### Transit-oriented development

Zoning reforms and density bonuses near transit hubs, with affordability requirements baked into the upzoning. The goal is to add housing precisely where infrastructure already exists.

### Prefabricated and modular housing

Looser regulations around prefabricated homes to accelerate construction timelines and reduce per-unit costs. Factory-built housing modules can be assembled on-site in weeks rather than months.

### Homeowner insurance

A **city-backed home insurance provider** to address the insurance crisis hitting small landlords and homeowners — rising premiums have been a major driver of rent increases independent of any RGB decision.

## How it's funded

| Source | Amount |
|--------|--------|
| City capital budget (5 years) | $22 billion |
| NYCHA improvements | $5.6 billion |
| **Total package** | **$28 billion** |

Additional funding is being pursued through federal grants (including negotiations with the White House over Sunnyside Yard) and innovative financing mechanisms like social bonds and land-value capture.

## Production context

NYC produced over **150,000 new housing units** from 2021–2025 — an unprecedented pace. Block by Block aims to maintain and accelerate that trajectory, but with a much higher share designated as permanently affordable. The plan explicitly rejects the framing that you must choose between "building more" and "protecting tenants" — it does both.

## What critics say

- **Fiscal conservatives**: $28 billion is massive; city debt service could crowd out other priorities.
- **Some housing advocates**: The plan still relies heavily on private developers and market mechanisms rather than a fully decommodified model.
- **NIMBY groups**: Transit-oriented density and upzoning face neighborhood opposition.

## What supporters say

- It's the most comprehensive housing production plan in NYC mayoral history.
- The $40/hour wage floor makes it a labor-backed plan, broadening the political coalition.
- Pairing production with the rent freeze creates a "protect + build" framework — stabilize existing tenants while expanding supply.
- The municipal development corporation gives the city a tool it's never had: the ability to build directly.
