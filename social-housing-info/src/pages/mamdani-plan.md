# Zohran Mamdani's Rent Freeze

On June 25, 2026, the NYC Rent Guidelines Board voted **7–1** to approve a **two-year rent freeze** — the first in the board's history. The freeze covers both one-year and two-year leases for roughly **one million rent-stabilized apartments** across the five boroughs, fulfilling a signature campaign pledge by Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

## What the freeze does

For leases beginning between **October 1, 2026 and September 30, 2027**, the RGB guideline increase is **0%**. That means:

- If you're renewing a one-year lease, your rent does not go up.
- If you're renewing a two-year lease, your rent does not go up for the full two-year term.
- If you're a new tenant moving into a vacant stabilized unit, your rent is based on the previous tenant's rent — with **no guideline increase** added (the HSTPA of 2019 eliminated the old 20% vacancy bonus).

## How many units are covered?

Approximately **one million apartments** — roughly **41% of NYC's rental units** and about **27% of all housing** in the city. Breakdown by borough:

| Borough | Stabilized Units |
|---------|-----------------|
| Brooklyn | ~279,000 |
| Bronx | ~233,000 |
| Manhattan | ~220,000 |
| Queens | ~180,000 |
| Staten Island | ~17,000 |

The stabilization system covers buildings with **six or more units built before 1974**, plus some buildings that receive tax abatements or government subsidies.

## Who does the freeze apply to?

**Every tenant in a rent-stabilized apartment** who signs a new or renewal lease during the freeze window (Oct 1, 2026 – Sept 30, 2027). It does not apply to:

- Market-rate (unregulated) apartments
- Rent-controlled units (the older, stricter system covering ~16,000 units)
- Public housing (NYCHA)
- Condos and co-ops

## Does the freeze transfer tenant-to-tenant?

**Yes — effectively.** When a tenant in a stabilized unit moves out, the unit **remains stabilized** (the 2019 HSTPA ended vacancy decontrol). And since the RGB set the guideline increase at 0% for the freeze year, a new tenant moving into a vacant stabilized unit pays the **same regulated rent** as the departing tenant — no vacancy bonus or increase applies.

Two important exceptions:

- **Preferential rent protection**: If the departing tenant was paying a preferential rent (below the legal regulated rent), the vacancy increase is calculated from the **preferential rent**, not the higher legal rent. This preserves affordability for the new tenant.
- **Succession rights**: A family member who has lived in the unit with the tenant for 2+ years (1 year for seniors or disabled persons) can take over the lease with **no rent increase**. A spouse can be added to the lease at any time.

## Can landlords still raise rent during the freeze?

**Yes, in limited ways.** The RGB freeze only applies to the annual **guideline increase** — the percentage the board votes on each year. Landlords can still apply for:

- **Major Capital Improvement (MCI) increases**: For building-wide improvements (new roof, boiler, plumbing). Capped at **2% per year** of the total cost. Requires approval from the Division of Housing and Community Renewal (DHCR). Under the 2019 HSTPA, MCI increases are removed from the rent after **30 years**.
- **Individual Apartment Improvement (IAI) increases**: For apartment-specific upgrades (new kitchen, bathroom). Capped at a total of **$15,000 over 15 years** (about $83-89/month), with a maximum of **3 IAIs per 15-year period**.

So during the 0% guideline year, your rent **can** still go up by up to 2% from an approved MCI, or by ~$89/month from an approved IAI. But the **automatic annual increase that usually happens** is zero.

## What were the normal increases before this?

The RGB sets increases every year. Recent history:

| Year | Mayor | 1-Year Lease | 2-Year Lease |
|------|-------|-------------|-------------|
| 2022 | Adams | 3.25% | 5.0% |
| 2023 | Adams | 2.75% | 3.2% (yr 1) / 3.2% (yr 2) |
| 2024 | Adams | 2.75% | 5.25% |
| 2025 | Adams | 3.0% | 4.5% |
| **2026** | **Mamdani** | **0%** | **0%** |

Over Eric Adams' four years, stabilized rents rose a total of about **12% on one-year leases**. Under Bill de Blasio's eight years, total increases were just **6%**.

## What happens after the two years?

The freeze is **not permanent**. For leases beginning after September 30, 2027, the RGB will vote again on the next year's guidelines. The board could set any percentage — including resuming increases. The freeze does not change the underlying law or the stabilization framework; it's a one-year (and two-year lease) decision by the board.

## How does this compare to past freezes?

Bill de Blasio achieved **three one-year rent freezes** (2015, 2016, and 2020), but those covered **only one-year leases**. Two-year leases still saw increases during those years. Mamdani's freeze is the **first to cover both one-year and two-year leases**, making it the most comprehensive freeze in the board's history.

## What critics say

- **Landlord groups** argue the freeze makes it harder to cover rising operating costs (insurance, property taxes, utilities) and will lead to deferred maintenance.
- **Some economists** warn that freezing rents on existing units while costs rise can reduce the quality of the housing stock over time.
- **Legal challenges** are expected — the real estate industry typically challenges RGB decisions in court.

## What supporters say

- Stabilized tenants are disproportionately **low-income, elderly, and people of color**. The median household income in a stabilized unit is about **$47,000**.
- NYC's rental vacancy rate is **~1.4%** — among the lowest in the country. Tenants have almost no bargaining power.
- The freeze doesn't prevent MCI or IAI increases, so genuine capital investments can still earn a return.
- Previous freezes under de Blasio did **not** cause a measurable decline in housing quality or supply.
