# Resources

## Academic research

- **Diamond, McQuade & Qian (2019)** — "The Effects of Rent Control Expansion on Tenants, Landlords, and Inequality: Evidence from San Francisco." *American Economic Review*. The most-cited recent study; found that SF's 1994 expansion benefited existing tenants but reduced supply. Important limitation: studies a first-generation hard-cap system.
  [doi.org/10.1257/aer.20181289](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20181289)

- **Goetz, Edward G., Anthony Damiano, Peter Hendee Brown, Patrick Alcorn & Jeff Matson (2021)** — "Minneapolis Rent Stabilization Study." Center for Urban and Regional Affairs (CURA), University of Minnesota. Found little empirical evidence that rent stabilization negatively impacts new construction. Modeled caps at 75% CPI, CPI, CPI+3%, and CPI+7%; even CPI+3% allowed returns at the 90th percentile. [cura.umn.edu](https://www.cura.umn.edu/research/minneapolis-rent-stabilization-study)

- **Autor, Palmer & Pathak (2014)** — "Housing Market Spillovers: Evidence from the End of Rent Control in Cambridge, Massachusetts." *Journal of Political Economy*. Found that decontrol in Cambridge raised property values but also increased crime.

- **Arnott, R. (1995)** — "Time for Revisionism on Rent Control?" *Journal of Economic Perspectives*. Classic paper arguing that second-generation rent stabilization can be efficient when paired with other policies.

## Policy & advocacy organizations

- **Community Service Society of New York** — Research and advocacy on housing affordability in NYC. [cssny.org](https://www.cssny.org/)
- **Tenants & Neighbors** — New York tenant advocacy organization. [tandn.org](https://www.tandn.org/)
- **National Low Income Housing Coalition** — Research on affordable housing policy nationwide. [nlihc.org](https://nlihc.org/)
- **Furman Center at NYU** — Data and research on housing and urban policy. [furmancenter.org](https://furmancenter.org/)

## Data sources

- **NYC Rent Guidelines Board** — Annual reports, income and expense data for stabilized buildings. [rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/)
- **NYU Furman Center Data Search** — Interactive data on NYC housing, including stabilization status by neighborhood. [furmancenter.org/data](https://furmancenter.org/data/)
- **American Community Survey (Census)** — National data on rental costs, income, and housing characteristics.

## New York laws

- **Rent Stabilization Law of 1969** (as amended) — The foundational statute.
- **Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974** — Extends stabilization to other NY municipalities.
- **Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019** — Major reforms eliminating vacancy decontrol and limiting rent increases.

## Further reading

- *Evicted* by Matthew Desmond — Pulitzer-winning ethnography of eviction and housing insecurity in America.
- *The Color of Law* by Richard Rothstein — How government policy created residential segregation; essential context for understanding why rent regulation is a racial equity issue.
- *Golden Gates* by Conor Dougherty — The housing crisis in the Bay Area and the fight over solutions.
