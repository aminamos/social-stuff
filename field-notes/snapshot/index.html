What the footprint includes

01

The electricity story is regional, not abstract.

AI runs inside data centers, and data centers draw from local grids. The strongest estimates point to rapid growth, but the climate effect depends on when and where that electricity is generated.

US Department of Energy · Berkeley Lab, 2024 ↗
02

Carbon intensity changes the answer.

A model query is not a fixed amount of emissions. Hardware efficiency, utilization, cooling, grid mix, and whether new demand is matched with clean power all move the result.

International Energy Agency · Energy and AI, 2025 ↗
03

Water is a siting and accounting question.

Cooling systems can consume water directly, while power generation can consume water indirectly. A responsible comparison must name the boundary: direct facility water, full supply chain water, or both.

US Department of Energy · FEMP ↗
04

The footprint starts before the prompt.

Chips, servers, buildings, transmission, and replacement cycles carry embodied impacts. Operational energy is visible; extraction and manufacturing are easier to miss.

UNEP · Global Resources Outlook 2024 ↗
05

Training data has a human cost too.

Environmental accounting is only one part of the question. Data licensing, consent, labor conditions, and who captures the value of a model belong in the same ledger.

US Copyright Office · AI and Copyright ↗
06

Use case matters more than ideology.

The relevant comparison is usually not “AI or nothing.” Compare the model-assisted workflow with the real alternative: a trip, a search session, a meeting, manual work, or another machine-learning system.

IEA · Energy and AI, sections on AI applications ↗

How to read a number

01

Name the boundary

Operational electricity, cooling water, embodied materials, and labor are different ledgers. Keep them separate.

02

Show the uncertainty

Per-query estimates vary by model, prompt, hardware, and location. Ranges beat false precision.

03

Compare the alternative

An impact claim needs a counterfactual: what would the person or organization do instead?
