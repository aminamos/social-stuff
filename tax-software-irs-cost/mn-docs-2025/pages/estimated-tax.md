# Estimated Tax

field\_block:node:page:body

You can pay Minnesota estimated tax any of these ways:

- [**Electronic funds transfer (Online Services)**](https://www.mndor.state.mn.us/tp/onlineservices/_/) \- It’s secure, easy, and convenient. You can make a single payment or schedule all four payments at once.
1. On the Landing page, select **Pay with a bank account**.
2. Select **No, I am not using a Letter ID**.
3. Select **Individual** for the Customer Type.
4. Select **Individual Income Tax** for the Tax Type.
5. Enter **Customer Information** and **Email Address**.
6. Select **Estimate** for the Type and Filing Period for Payment.
7. Complete the remaining fields.

**Note:** To cancel a payment, go to our [Online Services](https://www.mndor.state.mn.us/tp/onlineservices/_/) page and, under Other Resources, select **Search for a confirmation number**.

- **Credit or debit card** \- Use our [Online Services: Credit or Debit Card Payment System](https://www.mndor.state.mn.us/tp/onlineservices/_/?link=makepaymentcard) and US Bank will process the card payment.
- [**Check or money order**](https://www.mndor.state.mn.us/tp/onlineservices/_/?link=SendCheck) \- Create and print a payment voucher to include with your check or money order. Make your check payable to the Minnesota Department Revenue and include the last four digits of your Social Security Number in the memo line. Mail check and voucher to the address on the voucher. You may also contact us to request payment vouchers.

Keep a record of your payments. You will need this information when filing your return. Use our [payment record form](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2025-12/payment-record.pdf) to keep track of your payments.

[What is estimated tax? \[+\]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/estimated-tax#)

Estimated tax is how you pay tax on income not subject to withholding. Examples of this income include:

- Self-employment
- Pensions
- Unemployment compensation
- Interest and dividends
- Rents
- Alimony
- Gains from the sale of assets
- Prizes and awards

You may need to pay estimated tax if you do not have enough Minnesota income tax withheld from your salary, pension, or other income.

[Who should make estimated tax payments? \[+\]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/estimated-tax#)

You must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $500 or more in Minnesota income tax after subtracting your withholding and refundable credits. To avoid a penalty, your estimated tax payments plus your withholding and refundable credits must equal either of these:

- 90% of your current year’s original tax liability (66.7% if you are a farmer or commercial fisherman)
- 100% of your prior year’s total tax liability (110% if your federal adjusted gross income is more than $150,000)

If you are a nonresident or part-year resident, you must have had at least $1 of Minnesota tax liability to use 100% of your prior year’s tax.

[Due Dates for Estimated Tax Payments \[+\]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/estimated-tax#)

For most people, estimated tax payments are due four times a year:

| For the period: | Pay by: |
| --- | --- |
| Jan. 1 through March 31 | April 15​ |
| April 1 through May 31 | June 15 |
| June 1 through Aug. 31 | Sept. 15​ |
| Sept. 1 through Dec. 31 | Jan. 15 (of the following year) |

If you prefer to make one estimated tax payment for the full year, it is due April 15 of the current tax year.

If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the Minnesota Department of Revenue must receive your payment by the next business day.

**Note:** You do not need to make the January 15 payment if you file your Minnesota income tax return and pay the entire amount by January 31.

### Fiscal Year Taxpayers

Payments are due on the 15th day of the fourth, sixth, and ninth months of your fiscal year and the first month of your next fiscal year.

### Farmers and Commercial Fishermen

If at least two-thirds of your gross income is from farming or fishing, you only need to make one estimated payment on January 15 of the following year. You do not have to make this estimated payment if you file your return and pay your tax by March 1.

[Calculate Estimated Tax Payments \[+\]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/estimated-tax#)

1. Use federal Form 1040-ES, Estimated Tax for Individuals, to calculate your federal adjusted gross income.
2. Use [Form M1, Individual Income Tax](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2026-03/m1-25.pdf), to estimate your Minnesota tax liability and credits for the year.
3. Enter the federal adjusted gross income you calculated in Step 1 on Form M1, line 1.
4. Complete Form M1 using the Minnesota income tax rates for the current year and your filing status.
5. Divide the amount you owe by four to get each payment.

You may need to adjust your estimated payment amounts if tax laws change or if your income, deductions, exemptions, or credits change during the year.

[How can I find the amount of estimated tax payments made? \[+\]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/estimated-tax#)

If you did not keep records of your payments, you may contact us at 651-296-3781 or email us at [individual.incometax@state.mn.us](mailto:individual.incometax@state.mn.us) for a list of estimated tax payments made for any tax year. If you email us, include:

- First and Last Name
- Date of Birth
- Last 4 digits of Social Security Number
- Address

For tax practitioners, you must have an active [power of attorney](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/power-attorney) on file to make this request. Complete [Form REV184i](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2021-05/rev184_i.pdf) or Form [REV184b](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2019-11/rev184_b.pdf) for your client and submit this form with your request for estimated tax payment amounts. You may contact us at 651-296-3781 or email us at [individual.incometax@state.mn.us](mailto:individual.incometax@state.mn.us).

> **Note:** If you’ve previously made estimated tax payments or applied refunds toward future tax years, the department generally will not transfer or refund those payments until you file Form M1 for the tax year to which the payments were applied.
